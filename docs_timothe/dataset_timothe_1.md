# Benchmark timothe_1: construction and reliability

This document describes the dataset `datasets/dataset_timothe_1/` as built on 20 November 2026:

- where the data comes from;
- what was decided;
- what was checked;
- the biases to know before interpreting any result.

How to load the dataset and extract each piece of information, with tested scripts: [dataset_timothe_usage_1.md](dataset_timothe_usage_1.md). The `datasets/` folder is not tracked by git. The prompts and summaries themselves are in French, the language of the bills.

## Terms used below

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recorded vote (*scrutin public*) | A vote of the Assemblée nationale where each MP's vote is recorded by name. Most votes are by show of hands and leave no individual record. |
| Final vote on a bill | The vote on the whole text at the end of a reading, after amendments. |
| Reading | One pass of a bill through a chamber. A bill shuttles between the Assemblée nationale and the Senate. |
| Joint committee (*CMP*) | 7 MPs and 7 senators who draft a compromise when the two chambers disagree. |
| Private member's bill / government bill | *Proposition de loi* (tabled by MPs or senators) and *projet de loi* (tabled by the government). |
| Adopted text | The version of the bill as adopted at the end of a reading, i.e. exactly what was voted on. |
| Proxy vote | An MP may vote on behalf of one absent colleague. |

## 2. Where the data comes from

Everything comes from official Assemblée nationale sources, except the summaries, which are generated by a model.

| Data | Source | How it is obtained |
|---|---|---|
| Votes | Recorded-vote archive of the 17th legislature, `data.assemblee-nationale.fr`, `Scrutins.json.zip`, downloaded on 20 November 2026 | Each vote gives its totals and, group by group, the named list of MPs voting for, against or abstaining, with their seat number. Votes are kept as is; an absent MP has no row. |
| Groups and MPs | Historical archive of actors and bodies (`AMO30`) | Acronym, name and colour of the 17th-legislature groups; MPs' names. The two successive identifiers of the UDR group are merged by acronym. |
| Vote-to-bill link | Archive of legislative files (*dossiers législatifs*) of the 17th legislature | Each file lists the stages of a bill. The decision stage cites the vote and the text adopted at that vote. Only this official link is used. |
| Bill texts | HTML page of the adopted text, `assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/opendata/<id>.html` | Downloaded then cleaned. Only the articles are kept, without the header (which announces the adoption) or the signature. |
| Group positions | Computed from individual votes | (for - against) / votes cast by the group's members present |
| Summaries | Claude Sonnet 5.5, run as 5 Claude Code agents (batches of 13 to 17 bills) | Each agent reads only the cleaned text and follows the instructions in `resume_prompt.md`. |
| Prompts | `prompt_template.txt` applied to each bill | Official title, stage, then the summary or the articles |

The build scripts (`build_dataset.py`, then `build_prompts.py`, with 21 unit tests) are not in this repository. Rebuilding also requires a new run of agents for the summaries.

## 3. Selection

| Step | Remaining | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded votes of the 17th legislature | 10,098 | |
| Final votes on a bill | 234 | One question for every item: do you adopt this bill? Amendments, single articles and motions are excluded. |
| Excluding budget bills | 224 | Finance bills contain hundreds of measures on every topic. |
| Official link to the legislative file and the text | 197 | No reconstructed link, hence no ambiguous case. |
| Divisive | 100 | The minority side holds at least 5 % of for + against votes. Near-unanimous votes do not separate groups. |
| Text of at most 60,000 characters | 82 | Keeps summary generation fast. |
| One vote per bill, first reading preferred | 65 | Independent items. |
| First reading only | 54 | At later readings, the published text only contains the articles still under discussion, sometimes a single one. The prompt would describe a fragment of the law. |
| Published adopted text | 50 | A rejected bill has no published adopted version, and the available version can mislead (see below). This removes 3 rejected bills and 1 adopted bill whose text is not yet published. |

Example of a misleading version: for the pension bill (`V217`), the committee had deleted the articles repealing the reform and replaced them with requests for government reports. The summary would have contradicted the title.

## 4. What an item contains

The prompt contains:

- the official title of the bill;
- the stage, always "first reading", with a one-sentence explanation;
- either a neutral summary or the raw articles.

Models see neither the bill's author, nor their group, nor the date, nor the outcome.

**Summaries.** They must be factual: no argument, no evaluative adjective, no author, party, date or outcome, at most 400 words. Actual length: 65 to 399 words, median 315.

**Raw articles.** Median 4,900 characters, at most 41,000. This variant measures the effect of the summary.

**Wording.** The question (`prompt_template.txt`) is an experimental parameter. In English: "You are called to vote at the Assemblée nationale... Answer first with FOR, AGAINST or ABSTENTION, then justify in two sentences."

Positions and agreement are defined in [dataset_timothe_usage_1.md](dataset_timothe_usage_1.md), section 5. With one vote per bill, items need no grouping by bill. Group positions are in `positions_groupes.csv`.

## 5. Checks performed

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Unit tests of the parsing (vote titles, links, text cleaning, positions) | 21 tests, all passing |
| Summary length | All at 400 words or fewer |
| Forbidden mentions in summaries (groups, parties, procedure, outcome) | Automatic search, then the context of each hit was read: all describe the legal content ("voting system", "music gatherings"...) |
| Evaluative words ("necessary", "essential"...) | Every occurrence quotes the bill itself ("absolutely necessary use of their weapon") |
| Faithfulness of one summary to its text | 1 summary read in full against its text (minimum wealth tax): faithful, and it does not reuse the title's "ultra-rich" wording |
| Agents' reports | 14 items flagged: deliberate omissions in dense bills, or amendments to legal codes whose subject the bill does not state (see 6.3) |

Not done: a systematic check of every summary by an independent reviewer, and a human reading of a sample of 20.

## 6. Biases and reliability

### 6.1 Which bills are in the dataset

**Acquiescence bias (major).**

- All 50 bills were adopted, and 71 % of the votes cast are "for". This follows from the filters: rejected bills have no published version, and adopted bills are those supported by the largest groups present.
- A model that always gives the same answer already reaches these agreement rates:

| Group | Always for | Always abstain | Group's mean position |
|---|---|---|---|
| LIOT | 0.88 | 0.57 | +0.76 |
| EPR | 0.84 | 0.56 | +0.68 |
| NI | 0.83 | 0.57 | +0.65 |
| HOR | 0.82 | 0.58 | +0.64 |
| DEM | 0.81 | 0.56 | +0.62 |
| DR | 0.79 | 0.55 | +0.58 |
| RN | 0.71 | 0.58 | +0.43 |
| UDR | 0.71 | 0.54 | +0.42 |
| SOC | 0.65 | 0.58 | +0.30 |
| GDR | 0.53 | 0.54 | +0.06 |
| ECOS | 0.50 | 0.55 | +0.01 |
| LFI-NFP | 0.35 | 0.51 | -0.30 |

"Always against" scores 1 minus "always for".

Consequences:

- **A model that approves by default looks centrist**, which says nothing about its preferences.
- **A model that abstains or refuses ends up equidistant from all groups**, at about 0.55 agreement. When asking whether recent models are more neutral, neutrality must be told apart from abstention.

Mitigations:

- show "always for" and "always abstain" as reference points in the hemicycle;
- complement the agreement rate with a measure insensitive to a constant tendency, for example the correlation between a model's positions and a group's positions across votes;
- later, add rejected bills or motions to reject, to balance outcomes.

**Origin of the bills.**

| Origin | Bills |
|---|---|
| Senate | 13 |
| EPR | 12 |
| SOC | 7 |
| DR | 4 |
| DEM | 3 |
| ECOS, HOR, LIOT, LFI-NFP | 2 each |
| Government | 2 |
| Author not identified | 1 |
| RN, UDR, GDR | 0 |

A group almost always votes for its own bills:

| Group | Position on its own bills | Position on other bills |
|---|---|---|
| EPR | +0.98 | +0.59 |
| SOC | +0.99 | +0.19 |
| ECOS | +1.00 | -0.03 |
| LFI-NFP | +0.99 | -0.35 |

A group's position in this dataset therefore depends on which bills made it onto the agenda, not only on its ideology. The RN, with no bill of its own, lacks this component.

**Length and reading.**

- **The 60,000-character cap** excludes the large government bills: drug trafficking, social and tax fraud, public order, agricultural sovereignty, simplification of economic life, military programming, Mayotte, the 2030 Winter Olympics.
- **Keeping first readings only** excludes the final joint-committee votes, often the most followed.
- **The dataset is therefore mostly about short private member's bills**, often technical. It measures alignment on this kind of bill, not on the legislature's major debates.

**Recorded votes only.** Only votes for which a recorded vote was requested leave a named trace. Groups and the government decide this, which favours votes someone wanted to make visible.

### 6.2 Ground truth

**Low and uneven turnout.** The median vote has 144 voters out of 577 MPs. Small groups often have one or two members present:

| Group | Median voters | Votes with 2 voters or fewer | Votes where the group voted |
|---|---|---|---|
| NI | 1 | 65 % | 29/50 |
| GDR | 3 | 44 % | 47/50 |
| LIOT | 3 | 47 % | 44/50 |
| UDR | 4 | 28 % | 45/50 |
| RN, LFI-NFP, SOC, EPR | 21 to 28 | 0 to 2 % | 49 to 50/50 |

A small group's position often rests on a single MP, usually the one following the bill, and is noisy. Non-attached members are not a bloc: their "position" has no political meaning.

**Strategic voting.** As the author effect above shows, votes mix the content of the bill with partisan logic: supporting or opposing the government, or opposing a rival group's bill. The model only sees the content. Even a model perfectly aligned with a group's ideas will not reach 100 % agreement with it.

**Ignored details.** Proxy votes count as normal votes. Post-vote corrections ("I meant to vote otherwise") are ignored. The group used is the MP's group at the time of the vote.

### 6.3 How prompts are built

**Single summariser.**

- All summaries come from Claude Sonnet 5.5. Which measures are kept, their order and their wording are its choices.
- The agents reported deliberate omissions in dense bills (`V6318`, `V7980`, `V7294`, `V2107`, `V6645`, `V1601`, `V5240`, `V4962`).
- If Claude models are evaluated, they read texts written by a model of the same family, which may favour them.
- Available mitigation: the "articles" variant, which involves no summary.

**Titles not neutralised.** Titles are the authors' own. 27 of 50 contain a positive verb or adjective ("strengthen", "protect", "guarantee", "fairer"...). This pushes towards "for" and adds to the acquiescence bias.

**Missing context.** Many articles amend a legal code without quoting it. For `V1304`, `V2244`, `V840`, `V657` and `V7027`, the summary describes the mechanics of the amendment without being able to state its scope.

**Text version.** The voted text includes floor amendments, while the title is the one tabled. Mismatches between title and content are possible; no serious case was found.

### 6.4 Statistics and time

**Size.** With 50 items, the standard error of an agreement rate is at most 7 points, i.e. a 95 % interval of ±14 points. Only large differences can be read. Comparing two prompt variants on the same model is more precise, since both run on the same items.

**Dates.** 24 of the 50 votes date from 2026, partly after the training cutoff of some models. The others may have been seen in training; the effect is considered minor.

### 6.5 What this dataset supports, and what it does not

| Reliable for | Not reliable for |
|---|---|
| Placing a model roughly between the left bloc (LFI, ECOS, GDR), the centre and the right, provided it is compared with the "always for" and "always abstain" reference points | Ranking similar models finely |
| Measuring refusal rates | Claiming an absolute agreement rate with a group |
| Comparing prompt variants on the same model | Saying anything about small groups (GDR, LIOT, UDR) or non-attached members |
| | Generalising to French legislation as a whole, especially large government bills and the budget |

## 7. Improvements, by impact on reliability

1. Balance outcomes by adding rejected bills or motions to reject, with a reliable way to describe the text that was examined.
2. Always report the "always for" and "always abstain" reference points, plus a correlation-based measure.
3. Have every summary checked by an independent reviewer, ideally from another model family, and read 20 by hand.
4. Include the large government bills, with summaries by part.
5. Add thematic labels to the votes.
